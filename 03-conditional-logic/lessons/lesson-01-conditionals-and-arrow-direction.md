# Lesson 1 — Conditional Statements and Arrow Direction

A conditional expresses a relationship between a condition and an outcome.

`P → Q` means **if P, then Q**.

- P is the condition (antecedent).
- Q is the outcome (consequent).
- If P is true, Q must follow under the stated rule.

Example: If payment succeeds, the appointment is confirmed.

`PaymentSuccessful → AppointmentConfirmed`

This does not automatically mean payment success is the only way to confirm an appointment. A one-way rule must not be treated as a two-way rule.

## The converse

The converse of `P → Q` is `Q → P`. The original statement does not establish its converse.

If successful payment confirms an appointment, seeing a confirmed appointment does not, by itself, prove payment succeeded. Another process may have confirmed it.

## Read the direction carefully

- “Q if P” means `P → Q`.
- “If P, then Q” means `P → Q`.
- “Q whenever P” means `P → Q`.
- “Q provided that P” means `P → Q`.
- “Q only if P” means `Q → P`.

The phrase **only if** introduces a condition that must be present for the outcome. It is a common source of reversed arrows.

## Check your understanding

Translate: “A patient can book only if logged in.”

Answer: `CanBook → IsLoggedIn`. Being logged in is necessary for booking.

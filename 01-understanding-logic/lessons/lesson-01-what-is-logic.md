# Lesson 1: What Is Logic?

## Learning purpose
This lesson introduces logic as a way of thinking carefully about information before reaching a conclusion.

## Thinking and reasoning
**Thinking** is the broad mental activity of considering information, ideas, memories, questions, and possibilities.

**Reasoning** is a more focused kind of thinking in which we connect information and use it to reach or assess a conclusion.

For example, arriving at a clinic and seeing an empty reception area may make you wonder where the receptionist is. Reasoning means examining what you know before deciding why the receptionist is absent. The empty reception area alone does not tell you why nobody is there.

## Logic
**Logic** is the careful process of examining information and how ideas connect, so that conclusions are not based only on guesses.

Ask yourself:
- What do I actually know?
- What evidence supports this?
- What am I assuming?
- What else could explain the situation?
- Does my conclusion follow from the information?

## Why logic matters
When an appointment does not appear in a clinic application, it would be careless to immediately conclude that the patient never booked it. Separate the patient's report, available evidence, the application's current state, and possible explanations.

## Key takeaway
Do not jump from a problem directly to an explanation. First identify the information available, then examine what it supports.

# Lesson 1: Observation, Inference, and Conclusion

## Observation
An **observation** is what someone directly notices or records.

Example: You look at an appointment list and do not see a particular patient's appointment. This describes what was noticed; it does not explain why it happened.

## Inference
An **inference** is a possible explanation or further claim reached by reasoning from available information.

If an appointment is missing from the list, you might infer that it was not saved. But it could also have been cancelled, filtered out, or associated with a different patient record. An inference can be strong or weak, and it can be wrong.

## Conclusion
A **conclusion** is a claim reached after considering information and reasoning. It should not be stronger than the evidence supporting it.

## Possibilities and certainty
A **possibility** is an explanation that could be true. Identifying a possibility does not establish that it is likely or that it actually happened.

## Key takeaway
Describe what you observed, identify what you infer, and make clear what your evidence does and does not establish.

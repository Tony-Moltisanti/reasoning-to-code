# Milestone 2: Practice Exercises

1. In the student-ID argument (all registered students receive IDs; David is registered; therefore David receives an ID), identify premises and conclusion.
2. "All cats are birds; Max is a cat; therefore Max is a bird." Is the structure valid? Is the first premise true?
3. Every successful payment has a transaction reference. Payment 501 has a reference. Give a counterexample to the conclusion that payment 501 succeeded.
4. Every confirmed appointment must have a patient ID. Appointment 205 has no patient ID. What follows?
5. Explain the difference between an appointment confirmed at 9 AM and cancelled at 10 AM, and two simultaneous current records showing conflicting statuses.
6. Identify the fallacy: one booking succeeds, so the entire system is declared bug-free.
7. Identify the fallacy: failures follow a deployment, so the deployment is blamed without investigation.
8. Identify the fallacy: a system either works perfectly or must be abandoned.
9. Identify the fallacy: a feature works because it is functioning correctly.
10. Identify the fallacy: a claim is accepted solely because a senior developer said it.
11. A booking request displays "Booking successful" but no database record exists. State the strongest supported conclusion and a useful next investigation.
12. A screenshot shows a confirmed appointment, the database says cancelled, and another patient occupies the slot. List what is known, unknown, an unsupported assumption and the next useful evidence.

# Lesson 1: Premises, Conclusions, Truth, Validity and Soundness

A **premise** provides information or a reason supporting an argument. A **conclusion** is the claim reached from the premises.

Example: (1) All patients with confirmed appointments can proceed to consultation. (2) James has a confirmed appointment. (3) Therefore, James can proceed to consultation. Statements 1 and 2 are premises; statement 3 is the conclusion.

**Truth** asks whether a statement accurately describes reality. **Validity** asks whether the conclusion logically follows from the premises.

Example: All cats are birds; Max is a cat; therefore Max is a bird. The first premise is false in reality, but the conclusion follows if both premises are accepted. The argument is valid in structure.

An argument is **sound** when its reasoning is valid and all its premises are true. A valid argument with a false premise is unsound.

**Software connection:** Code can implement a rule correctly while the underlying business rule is wrong. Check both the logic and the correctness of the rules.

# Lesson 4: Five Common Reasoning Fallacies

A **fallacy** is a mistake in reasoning that makes an argument weak or unreliable.

1. **Hasty generalisation:** drawing a broad conclusion from too little evidence. One successful booking does not prove the entire system is bug-free.
2. **False cause:** assuming one event caused another because they occurred together or one followed the other. Failures after a deployment do not, by timing alone, prove the deployment caused them.
3. **False dilemma:** presenting only two choices when more exist. A system need not be either perfect or a complete failure.
4. **Circular reasoning:** using the conclusion itself as support. Saying a feature works because it is functioning correctly adds no independent evidence.
5. **Appeal to authority:** treating someone's position or expertise as sufficient proof. A senior developer's confidence does not replace verification.

**Testing connection:** Ask what evidence supports a claim, whether the reasoning follows, and what alternative explanations need checking.
